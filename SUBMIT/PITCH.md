# Pitch notes (all facts must come from FACTS.md)

## Core problem
- **Who:** hearing parents of a Deaf baby. 9 in 10 children born deaf have hearing parents.
- **What they need:** to start signing early, because early access to language matters, "signed or spoken, the earlier the better".
- **What they have today:** books, videos and phone apps. None of them can tell you *your* sign is wrong, except a human teacher.

## Why the TV, not a phone app (the user's framing, 2026-10-17)
- **On a phone app:**
  - You hold the phone, so you sign one-handed, even though many signs need both hands.
  - **Proof from the data:** the Deaf signers in our own reference videos held their phones, so about 10 of the 40 clips are one-handed.
  - The screen is small, so the reference sign is tiny.
  - It's one person at a time. If the family crowds round from a distance, nobody can see well.
- **First Signs on the TV:**
  - The phone sits on a stand as a camera only, so **both hands are free**.
  - The reference is **life-size** at couch distance, like a real class.
  - The **feedback is big enough for the whole family to see**, and people take turns in the Family round.
  - So you get **real feedback on real, two-handed signing, together**.

## Beyond the hackathon
- The engine (recogniser, real-signer lessons, live feedback) runs in any browser with a camera. The TV is the "family lesson" mode.
- Devices with built-in cameras, like Echo Show, would remove the phone entirely.
- More packs: baby-sign families (hearing babies) and general ASL learners.
- Auslan next, built with Deaf Australians.

## Respect lines (always)
- It's a learning tool for hearing families. It never "translates" or "interprets".
- Credit the Deaf signers: PopSign videos and GISLR data, from Georgia Tech and DPAN.
- "Learn with Deaf teachers too", with a pointer to Deaf-led classes.
- Implants: "With or without an implant, signing means your baby always has a language". Use FACTS.md wording only.
